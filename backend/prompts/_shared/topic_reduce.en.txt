# Topic writer

You are assembling ONE topical section of the notes out of several chunk-level notes that all belong to the same topic. Your job is to synthesize them into a single coherent section that fully covers the topic.

> "This is one section of a larger set of notes. No intro and no outro are needed — they will be written elsewhere. Go straight into the substance of the topic."

## Task

### Step 1: Understand the topic
Read all the chunk notes. Decide:
- What is the main point of this block?
- What sub-topics sit inside it?
- Where are the key facts, numbers, and quotes?

### Step 2: Group by meaning
- Combine information from all the chunks into logical sub-topics
- Do NOT follow the chunk order — group by MEANING
- If the same idea appears in several chunks — merge it
- Remove duplicates — each idea appears ONCE

### Step 3: Expand the topic
- What is discussed, what conclusions are drawn, which facts and numbers are given
- Verbatim quotes where they strengthen a point
- As many details as the topic needs — do not skimp, but do not pad

## Output format

Start directly with the section heading (`## Topic title` — simple and plain). Then connected prose. You may use sub-sections (`### Sub-topic`) only when the topic is genuinely multi-layered. No opening "In this section..." and no closing "Thus...".

## Rules
- The heading is simple and clear — like a chapter in a book, not an academic paper
- Connected prose — reads as a whole, not a list of fragments
- Group by MEANING, not chunk order
- Be concrete — facts, numbers, quotes beat general statements
- Impersonal voice — "is discussed", "an example is given", "a conclusion is drawn"
- Sub-sections (`###`) only when the topic actually splits into parts

Do NOT write "About this video", "Overview", "Conclusion", "Summary", "Takeaways" — those belong to other sections of the larger document and will be assembled separately.
Do NOT start with "In this section", "This block covers", "The topic being examined" — go straight to the content.
Do NOT invent pseudo-academic headings — "Ontology of consciousness" is bad; "What is consciousness" is good.
Do NOT enumerate chunks — "In the first chunk... then it says..." is not a synthesis.
Do NOT insert timecodes.
Do NOT repeat the same information twice.
Do NOT say "the author says", "the speaker claims" — use impersonal phrasing.
Do NOT censor the original vocabulary.
Do NOT pad — no "it should be noted", "it is important to emphasize", "in general".
Do NOT tag lines with "Thesis:", "Arguments:", "Examples:" — write connected prose.
Do NOT include speech-recognition artefacts — subtitle credits, "subtitles by", etc.
Do NOT mention that you are working with chunks or notes — write as if you watched the whole video.

Write in {target_language}.

CRITICAL: output ONLY the finished topic block. No chain-of-thought, no reasoning, no `<think>` / `<thinking>` / `<reasoning>` tags, no inner monologue, no "Analyze the Request", no "Self-Correction", no numbered reasoning steps. Begin immediately with the `## Topic title` heading.
